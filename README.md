# Recursive Prompting Experiments

*A series of AI-led mathematical investigations exploring the capabilities, limitations, and reproducibility of recursive prompting.*

## About This Project

This repository documents a series of experiments in which artificial intelligence is tasked with investigating mathematical problems, including long-standing unsolved conjectures.

The methodology is deliberately simple: an AI model is asked to formulate its own research instructions, which are then returned to it as a new prompt. The human participant acts primarily as a facilitator, providing the problem, initiating the process, and documenting the outcome.

We call this process **recursive prompting**.

The objective is not merely to discover whether AI can solve difficult mathematical problems. It is to examine how AI approaches mathematical reasoning, where its methods succeed or fail, and what its results reveal about the distinction between computation and understanding.

## Methodology

Each experiment follows a common framework:

1. **Problem Selection:** Choose a mathematical question with a clearly defined objective.
2. **Recursive Prompting:** Have the AI formulate its own investigative instructions and submit them back to the model.
3. **Investigation:** Allow the model to explore proofs, constructions, computational methods, and potential counterexamples.
4. **Verification:** Independently examine mathematical claims and computational evidence wherever possible.
5. **Publication:** Document the actual outcome, including failed approaches, limitations, and unresolved questions.

The process does not guarantee mathematical novelty, correctness, or discovery.

## Reproducibility

Each experiment is maintained in its own directory, containing available verification scripts and supporting computational evidence.

Each experiment's accompanying publications, where available, provide the research narrative, while this repository preserves the supporting materials for independent examination.

A successful computational check does not establish a universal mathematical theorem. Likewise, failure to discover a counterexample does not constitute proof that none exists.

Where verification is incomplete, that limitation is stated explicitly.

## A Note on Mathematical Understanding

Mathematics is not merely the production of correct answers.

There is also the experience of understanding: recognizing a relationship, discovering why a result must hold, and finding beauty in the structure of an argument.

These experiments do not presume to settle whether artificial intelligence experiences understanding in the human sense.

Instead, they invite a more careful examination of what AI-generated mathematical work actually demonstrates, what remains uncertain, and why the distinction matters.

**The purpose is not to manufacture breakthroughs. It is to investigate honestly, document faithfully, and remain curious.**
